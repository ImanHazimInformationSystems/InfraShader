## Fairfax package: what each reviewed page is

- p.1: agency memorandum and committee agenda (owner document).
- pp.2–3: board agenda item (issue, recommendation, timing, background, fiscal impact, enclosures, staff).
  This is the owner decision record.
- p.8: the owner's cost estimate table. Lines are split by responsibility (authority versus requesting
  association), with subtotals and a total project cost. The association's line is taken from the
  contractor contract.
- **pp.9–11: a genuine completed contractor proposal** from a landscape contractor to a client
  association, dated 7 Sep 2017. It is signed by the contractor and the client block is blank. It is not
  a blank template.
- p.12: a supplier engineering drawing (plan view, keyway detail, scales, P.E. stamp), attached as a
  technical appendix.

## Structure of the completed proposal (pp.9–11)

1. Letterhead with trade-association memberships and a contractor licence number; footer with office
   addresses.
2. Date, then **Client** and **Job Site** address blocks.
3. Opening scope statement: furnish labour, materials and equipment. Work is limited to the areas on the
   owner's plans *identified by date and sheet number*, installed to a named industry specification.
4. **Scope of Work**: a narrative description of the item with dimensions and product source. It states
   what is included and, in bold inline, what is not (for example footing concrete/steel, an access road
   by others, backfill material supplied by the owner).
5. A bold disclaimer: prices are for convenience and any **quantity change requires re-pricing**.
6. A single lump-sum line: "Furnish labor and materials" with the amount. No unit-rate breakdown is shown
   to the client.
7. **Clarifications**: a bullet list of exclusions and work by others (clearing, grading, fencing, stake
   out, permits, geotechnical fees, temporary access road and its restoration).
8. **Standard terms** lettered a–k: watering and maintenance handover, plant warranty, scheduling and
   delay, site conditions on receipt, insurance, title until payment, change orders in writing, payment
   terms with deposit, law/venue, non-solicitation, and **proposal validity (30 days)**.
9. A two-party **acceptance block** (signature, printed name/title, date).

## Lessons applied to template v4 (inspiration, not requirements)

- Identify the drawings and data the price is based on (InfraShader: design revision, option, site
  evidence and plan sheet), and state that quantity changes require a new priced revision. This matches
  the existing K02/L26 revision rules.
- Give "Not included / work by others" its own prominent section, separate from assumptions. The
  reference treats it as its main risk control.
- The client-facing price can be a summary (lump sum or grouped sections) while the itemised schedule sits
  in a technical appendix. This supports the concise proposal plus detailed appendix modes from the same
  approved data. Internal costs and supplier detail stay out of the client output.
- Validity period, programme/scheduling assumptions, maintenance/watering handover and warranty
  responsibility are user-entered commercial terms. InfraShader must not supply legal defaults.
- The owner-side record (pp.2–3, p.8) splits cost by responsibility and links to the contractor contract.
  This is relevant to the separate owner decision output (O17).
- A signature/acceptance block is legally significant. Per the brief it is **not** added as an incidental
  UI action; any electronic acceptance needs separate scope and approval.
